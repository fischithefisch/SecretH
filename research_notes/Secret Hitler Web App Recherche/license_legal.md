## 1. What license covers Secret Hitler, what is covered, and what does secrethitler.com say?

### Takeaway
Secret Hitler (rules plus print-and-play files) is released under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. The license covers copyrighted material (rules text, artwork, card and board designs) and **explicitly does not license trademark rights**. The name "SECRET HITLER" is a registered US trademark of Goat, Wolf & Cabbage LLC. secrethitler.com adds a plain-language summary that includes an **"app store" prohibition** that is stricter than the license text itself.

### Cited Findings
- A free print-and-play edition was released on **25 Nov 2015** under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The retail game is from Goat, Wolf & Cabbage LLC, designed by Max Temkin, Mike Boxleiter and Tommy Maranges, with art by Mackenzie Schubert, first released 25 Aug 2016. — [Wikipedia (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Hitler)
- Plain-language license statement as it appears on secrethitler.com, reproduced verbatim in a fan web implementation's README: *"Secret Hitler is licensed under Creative Commons BY–NC–SA 4.0. That means you have to give credit for the original, you're not allowed to profit from it commercially in any way, and you have to license it under the exact same CC license. You also can't submit anything to an app store or anything like that."* — [kaelri/secret-hitler README](https://github.com/kaelri/secret-hitler). The same wording shows up in search summaries attributed to [secrethitler.com](https://www.secrethitler.com/). Direct fetch of secrethitler.com was blocked, so I could not confirm the exact current wording or its location on the page.
- A search summary also reports that self-made print-and-plays are allowed "as long as they attribute the original creator Goat Wolf & Cabbage, are not sold for profit, and are released under the same license". The underlying source page could not be identified exactly. — [search result set incl. secrethitler.com / BGG P&P version page](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgameversion/301129/english-print-and-play-edition)
- CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code, Sec. 2(b)(2): *"Patent and trademark rights are not licensed under this Public License."* — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code, copy in cozuya/secret-hitler LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- Same license, Sec. 2(b)(1): moral rights and publicity/personality rights are not licensed, though the licensor waives them to the extent needed for the licensed uses. — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- "Licensed Material means the artistic or literary work, database, or other material to which the Licensor applied this Public License." — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- Trademark: "SECRET HITLER", US Serial No. 87281991, filed 27 Dec 2016 by Goat, Wolf & Cabbage LLC for board and card games, registered 18 Jul 2017. — [Justia Trademarks (via search summary)](https://trademarks.justia.com/872/81/secret-87281991.html); [Goat, Wolf & Cabbage LLC trademark list](https://trademark.justia.com/owners/goat-wolf-cabbage-llc-3459398)

### Inferences
- What the license covers: rules text and the visual assets in the print-and-play files (card and board graphics, role art, policy tiles). Game mechanics as such are generally not protected by copyright, so a re-implementation of the rules with your own text and art would not even need the license. The NC/SA conditions only become relevant once original texts or images are copied or adapted. This reflects general copyright doctrine; I found no specific source for it in this research.
- The name/trademark is not licensed by CC. In practice, fan sites such as secrethitler.io use the name openly, and I found no evidence of enforcement against non-commercial web versions (see Q4). The legal basis for that is tolerance, not a license.
- The "no app store" line is a request/interpretation by the licensor on the website, not a clause in the CC legal code. For a private web app (no app store) it does not apply, but it shows the creators are wary of distribution channels that look commercial.

## 2. What do the BY / NC / SA obligations mean in practice for a web app?

### Takeaway
**BY:** credit creators, link to the license, link to the original, and say what was changed. **NC:** no use "primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation". Ads and paid tiers are risky, while cost-recovery donations are a grey area. **SA:** applies to *Adapted Material*, meaning modified assets and rules texts, which must again be released under BY-NC-SA (or a later version). It does **not** automatically force the app's program code under CC, but many fan projects put everything under BY-NC-SA anyway. If you never "Share" (publicly provide) the material, most obligations are not triggered, though a password-free public URL counts as sharing.

### Cited Findings
- Attribution requirements when sharing (also in modified form), Sec. 3(a)(1): retain (i) identification of the creator(s) "in any reasonable manner requested by the Licensor", (ii) a copyright notice, (iii) a notice referring to the license, (iv) a notice referring to the disclaimer of warranties, (v) a URI/hyperlink to the Licensed Material where reasonably practicable. Also (b) indicate whether you modified it and keep any earlier modification notices, and (c) indicate the CC license and include its text or a URI. — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- Sec. 3(a)(2): conditions may be met "in any reasonable manner based on the medium, means, and context … it may be reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required information." Sec. 3(a)(3): on the licensor's request, attribution must be removed. — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- "Share means to provide material to the public by any means or process … including in ways that members of the public may access the material from a place and at a time individually chosen by them." — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- NonCommercial: *"not primarily intended for or directed towards commercial advantage or monetary compensation."* — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- ShareAlike, Sec. 3(b): if you share **Adapted Material**, the Adapter's License must be a CC license with the same elements (BY-NC-SA), this version or later, or a compatible license. You must include it or link to it, and you may not add terms or DRM that restrict the rights granted. — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- "Adapted Material" = material "derived from or based upon the Licensed Material … translated, altered, arranged, transformed, or otherwise modified in a manner requiring permission under the Copyright". "Adapter's License" = the license you apply to "Your Copyright and Similar Rights in Your contributions to Adapted Material". — [same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- Creative Commons recommends **against** using CC licenses for software, because they have no source-code terms, do not deal with patents, and are not compatible with major software licenses. Exception: BY-SA 4.0 is one-way compatible with GPLv3. — [CC FAQ (archived copy, via search summary)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/dentonfracking/20141030045305mp_/http://wiki.creativecommons.org/FAQ)
- CC's "Defining Noncommercial" study (2009): creators and users generally rate online advertising as "commercial". A not-for-profit using ads only to cover hosting scored 59.2 and 71.7 (out of 100 = "definitely commercial") among US respondents. Global respondents rated ads-as-cost-recovery somewhat less commercial (35.7 / 40.3). — [Creative Commons blog: Defining Noncommercial report](https://creativecommons.org/2009/09/14/defining-noncommercial-report-published/)
- Practice of the largest fan web app (secrethitler.io, repo cozuya/secret-hitler): the repo's LICENSE file is the full CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 text, so the whole repo, code included, is under BY-NC-SA. The README section "License and Attribution" reads: *"Secret Hitler is designed by Max Temkin (Cards Against Humanity, Humans vs. Zombies) Mike Boxleiter (Solipskier, TouchTone), Tommy Maranges (Philosophy Bro) and illustrated by Mackenzie Schubert (Letter Tycoon, Penny Press). This game is licensed as per the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International license."* There is also a section "Alterations to the original game": *"Minor image alterations and editing - assets available in this repository."* plus rule tweaks (veto handling). — [cozuya/secret-hitler README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/README.md); [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- Another fan web implementation (kaelri/secret-hitler, Express/Vue/Socket.IO) only states the license summary and links to the original. — [kaelri/secret-hitler](https://github.com/kaelri/secret-hitler)
- A fan print-and-play extension (reitzig/secret-hitler-extended) credits "Based on the original print & play off secrethitler.com" and releases its additions under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. — [reitzig/secret-hitler-extended](https://github.com/reitzig/secret-hitler-extended)

### Inferences
- **Practical attribution template** (derived from Sec. 3(a), mirroring secrethitler.io). Put it in a footer or "About/Credits" page:
  "Based on *Secret Hitler* by Max Temkin, Mike Boxleiter, Tommy Maranges; illustrated by Mackenzie Schubert; published by Goat, Wolf & Cabbage LLC (secrethitler.com). Licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (link). Changes: [e.g., digitized/cropped artwork, rules adapted for online play]. This fan project is not affiliated with or endorsed by the creators." The "not affiliated" line is not required by the license, but it lowers trademark/endorsement risk.
- **SA and code:** the legal code ties ShareAlike to *Adapted Material* derived from the licensed work, meaning assets and rules text. Whether a game engine that only *displays* CC images is an "adaptation" of them is legally unclear. The usual reading is that separate software is not an adaptation of images it loads, but bundling and visual integration blur the line. Safest options: (a) put the asset folder and any adapted rules text under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and the code under a normal software license (MIT/GPL); or (b) follow secrethitler.io and put everything under BY-NC-SA. If the code is not published at all (private hobby), the SA obligation in practice only covers the adapted assets that are made public.
- **Private use:** if the app runs only for a closed group of friends (login/password, unlisted and not indexed), it is arguably not "Share" to "the public", so the BY/SA conditions are not even triggered. That depends on how German copyright law treats "Öffentlichkeit" (public), a point not covered in this research. Adding attribution anyway costs nothing and is recommended.
- **NC in practice:** no ads, no paid accounts, no Patreon or tip jar tied to the game. A voluntary donation button purely for server costs is a grey area. It is probably tolerated, but the licensor's own summary says "not allowed to profit from it commercially in any way", so leaving donations out entirely is the safe choice. App-store distribution (even free) is out, per the licensor's request.

### Gaps
- No official CC 4.0 FAQ text on "is software that uses CC images an Adaptation?" could be retrieved (creativecommons.org blocked). The software statement above comes from an older archived FAQ copy.
- No German case law on CC NC/SA for game fan projects was researched.

## 3. Trademark, creators' statements, and how secrethitler.io handled it

### Takeaway
"SECRET HITLER" is a registered US trademark (2017) of Goat, Wolf & Cabbage. I found **no public statement by Temkin, Boxleiter or Maranges specifically about secrethitler.io or online fan versions**, beyond the general CC/no-app-store statement. secrethitler.io has run since fall 2016 under the name, with CC attribution and no visible special permission. Its long existence suggests tolerance, but I found no documented permission.

### Cited Findings
- US trademark "SECRET HITLER", Ser. No. 87281991, owner Goat, Wolf & Cabbage LLC, filed 27 Dec 2016, registered 18 Jul 2017, for board/card games. — [Justia (via search summary)](https://trademarks.justia.com/872/81/secret-87281991.html)
- secrethitler.io describes itself as "A web adaptation of the social deduction board game Secret Hitler, originally developed in early fall 2016", and its README contains only the CC attribution. No mention of a permission or license agreement. — [cozuya/secret-hitler](https://github.com/cozuya/secret-hitler); [README raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/README.md)
- Press summary: creators released the game under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, "which means that the title's rules and design can be remixed and adapted by fans – as long as they don't attempt to charge for their custom-made versions" (Tabletop Gaming, Feb 2017, on the Harry Potter reskin "Secret Voldemort"). — [Tabletop Gaming (via search summary)](https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/news/this-fan-made-harry-potter-re-skin-of-secret-hitler-swaps-fascists)
- A targeted search for a Temkin statement on secrethitler.io or online versions found nothing specific. — [search result set, e.g. Roll Call 2017](https://www.rollcall.com/2017/02/27/senators-get-secret-hitler-game/)
- A Max Planck Institute for Human Development (MPIB Berlin) research platform ("Secret-Hitler vs AI") runs on a secrethitler.io dev instance. That suggests academic institutions in Germany also operate secrethitler.io-based servers; the page content could not be fetched. — [MPIB about page (search result only)](https://secret-hitlerio-dev.chm.mpib-berlin.mpg.de/about)

### Inferences
- Trademark law (US, and in DE/EU only if registered or used there) protects use "in the course of trade" (geschäftlicher Verkehr). A strictly private, non-commercial friends' app is very unlikely to be trademark use in the course of trade, so the trademark risk is low. It rises with a public domain name like "secrethitler-xyz.de", ads, or anything that suggests an official product.
- Using the original name in a public app is tolerated by the rights holders in practice (secrethitler.io, many GitHub repos) but not licensed. Renaming removes this residual risk completely.

### Gaps
- No documented statement by the creators about online versions, and no evidence of whether secrethitler.io got informal permission.
- EU/DE trademark registration status unknown (see Q1).
- Max Temkin left Cards Against Humanity in 2020 after misconduct allegations. Whether this changed anything at Goat, Wolf & Cabbage or in its licensing stance was not researched.

## 5. Germany: §86a StGB, the name "Hitler", artwork, German edition, renamed clones

### Takeaway
§86a StGB criminalizes the **use of symbols (Kennzeichen)** of unconstitutional or Nazi organizations: flags, badges, uniforms, slogans, greetings, and especially the swastika and SS runes. **The original Secret Hitler artwork deliberately contains no swastikas or other Nazi symbols and no depiction of Hitler.** Characters are anthropomorphic animals, with fascists as reptiles, and the game is sold in Germany (eBay.de etc.). The name "Hitler" by itself is not a "Kennzeichen" within §86a, so §86a risk with the original assets is low. The bigger issues are taste and social acceptance (store removals abroad), and the risk of user-generated content (chat, custom avatars) bringing in actual Nazi symbols. I found no localized German edition from a German publisher. Rethemes ("Secret Voldemort", "Secret Hydra", "Secret Sith/Palpatine") exist mainly because of the theme's controversy, not because of German law.

### Cited Findings
- §86a forbids the use of "symbols, flags, insignia, uniforms, slogans, propaganda, and greetings" of unconstitutional organizations. Penalty up to 3 years imprisonment or a fine. — [Wikipedia: Strafgesetzbuch section 86a (via search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strafgesetzbuch_section_86a)
- Exemptions: §86a(3) refers to §86(4) [after the 2021 renumbering; previously §86(3)], the social adequacy clause covering art, science, research, teaching, civic education, reporting on history and current events, and similar. The art exemption only applies to conduct within the sphere of artistic creation (BayObLG, 29 Nov 2023, 202 StRR 88/23). — [BayObLG decision (gesetze-bayern.de, via search summary)](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/Y-300-Z-BECKRS-B-2023-N-35650?hl=true); [Bundestag research paper "Kennzeichen verfassungswidriger Organisationen" (search result)](https://www.bundestag.de/resource/blob/1118130/kennzeichen-verfassungswidriger-organisationen.pdf)
- Since Aug 2018, the USK considers the social adequacy clause for **computer games**, so games with swastikas can be rated if they deal critically with the context. Board games with heavy swastika use had been unsellable in Germany, and toys with swastikas have been seized in individual cases. — [Notebookcheck (via search summary)](https://www.notebookcheck.com/Verfassungsfeindliche-Symbole-in-Spielen-nicht-mehr-generell-verboten.321328.0.html); [bpb "Spiele mit Hakenkreuzen" (search result)](https://www.bpb.de/themen/kultur/digitale-spiele/504561/spiele-mit-hakenkreuzen-zum-umgang-mit-ideologie-und-vergangenheit/); [Slate 2018](https://slate.com/technology/2018/08/germany-lifted-its-ban-on-nazi-imagery-in-video-games-thats-great.html)
- Secret Hitler is set in an alternative 1930s Germany "where all the humans are anthropomorphised animals" and "does not include any Nazi symbols or images of Hitler". — [Tabletop Gaming release article (via search summary)](https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/news/find-the-fuhrer-party-game-secret-hitler-released-then-immediately)
- Statement on a Q&A site: the official game "does not use any particular references to the Nazi party", and the designer "intentionally removed any direct references and symbology of the Nazi party". Secondary/low-authority source. — [Quora (via search summary)](https://www.quora.com/Is-it-illegal-to-play-Secret-Hitler-in-Germany-or-other-countries-that-banned-Nazi-symbols)
- Fascists are portrayed as reptiles, with Hitler as a dinosaur-like creature (search summary, source among Secret Voldemort/Hydra results; exact source not identifiable). — [search result set, e.g. Brisca substack](https://briscacuba.substack.com/p/por-que-secret-voldemort-es-uno-de)
- The English game is sold in Germany via eBay.de (around €14–24). I found no German-language edition from a German publisher. Several eBay listings use odd spellings like "Secret H1tler", which may point to platform keyword filtering. — [eBay.de listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/286865658182); [eBay.de listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/197815943277)
- Rethemes: "Secret Voldemort" (Harry Potter: Death Eaters vs. Order of the Phoenix), "Secret Hydra" (Marvel: Hydra vs. SHIELD, "to make the game more socially acceptable"), "Secret Sith", "Secret Palpatine". Reasons given: the theme "could have been considered too controversial", plus the CC license allowing remixing. — [search summary incl. Tabletop Gaming](https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/news/this-fan-made-harry-potter-re-skin-of-secret-hitler-swaps-fascists); [Nerdist: "Take Secret Hitler Out of Germany and Into Your Favorite IPs With These Rethemes"](https://nerdist.com/article/take-secret-hitler-out-of-germany-and-into-your-favorite-ips-with-these-rethemes/); [MakerWorld "Secret Sith"](https://makerworld.com/en/models/2109415-secret-sith-board-game); [SecretHydra Chrome extension](https://chromeboard.com/extension/secrethydra-objdabhlkmnadgkbngknoogfhcgimdok); [BGG list of rethemes](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2345090/links-to-all-known-secret-hitler-variants-and-reth)

### Inferences
- §86a targets *Kennzeichen*. A personal name in a game title is not a symbol, flag, badge, uniform, slogan or greeting. Using "Hitler" as a role name or title is therefore very likely **not** §86a. Slogans like "Heil Hitler" or "Sieg Heil" **would** be, so don't put such phrases in UI text, sounds or bot chat. According to the sources above, the original art (anthropomorphic animals, with fascists as reptiles) contains no Nazi symbols. I could not verify the details of the fascist-side iconography, such as skull or eagle motifs. Caveat: I could not visually inspect the assets in this research, so the final report should recommend a quick check of each asset for swastikas, SS runes, Reichsadler-with-swastika or similar before use.
- User-generated content is the realistic risk: a chat or custom player names/avatars could be used to post Nazi symbols. For a friends-only app this is low, but a word filter or private rooms make sense.
- Even if legal, the Hitler theme can feel bad in a German social context (colleagues, mixed groups). That is a social rather than legal reason many German groups prefer rethemes.
- Rethemes using other franchises (Harry Potter, Star Wars, Marvel) *add* third-party IP risk (see the "Secret Palpatine" DMCA). A neutral, self-invented theme (e.g., "Secret Dictator", fantasy or sci-fi setting) avoids both the Hitler issue and the third-party IP issue.

### Gaps
- No German court decision or authority statement on Secret Hitler specifically. No official German edition (Kosmos/Pegasus/etc.) found; it is likely that none exists, but this is unconfirmed.
- Full text of §86/§86a StGB and the Bundestag paper could not be fetched (egress blocked). The paragraph references above come from search summaries and court-decision snippets. Exact current numbering should be checked against gesetze-im-internet.de.
- The exact contents of the original artwork (whether any eagle or emblem resembles the Reichsadler) were not visually verified.
- I found no specific source for a retheme named exactly "Secret Dictator". It seems to be used generically by some fan implementations but is not confirmed.

## 6. Practical options for a hobby developer in Germany (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Three viable paths with falling legal residual risk: (A) original name and assets with full CC attribution, non-commercial, private → legally acceptable, small trademark/taste risk; (B) original name, self-drawn neutral art → no CC obligations for the art, rules adapted in your own words; (C) renamed, self-drawn retheme → minimal risk. Under any option: no money, no app store, credits page.

### Cited Findings
- License obligations and NC definition as quoted above. — [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 legal code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/LICENSE)
- Licensor's plain-language conditions incl. "can't submit anything to an app store". — [kaelri/secret-hitler README quoting secrethitler.com](https://github.com/kaelri/secret-hitler)
- Precedent for option A: secrethitler.io (since 2016, CC BY-NC-SA for everything, credits in the README, "minor image alterations"). — [cozuya/secret-hitler](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cozuya/secret-hitler/master/README.md)
- Precedent for option C: rethemes such as Secret Voldemort and Secret Hydra. — [Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/take-secret-hitler-out-of-germany-and-into-your-favorite-ips-with-these-rethemes/)

### Inferences
- **Option A (original assets + name):** allowed under CC BY-NC-SA if non-commercial. Requirements: credit (designers, illustrator, Goat Wolf & Cabbage, link to secrethitler.com), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 link, note on changes, modified assets under BY-NC-SA. Don't publish in app stores, no ads or donations. Residual risks: trademark (low if private and non-commercial), the creators' theme sensitivity, and a final check of assets for §86a symbols.
- **Option B (own art, original name):** game mechanics free, own art under any license. Rules text still needs attribution and BY-NC-SA if adapted from the original wording. The name remains a trademark issue (low risk privately).
- **Option C (rename + own art, e.g. a neutral "Secret Dictator"/fantasy theme):** if no original text or art is copied, CC obligations largely disappear. A voluntary "inspired by Secret Hitler (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" credit is good practice and required if rules wording is adapted. Avoid third-party franchises (Harry Potter, Star Wars, Marvel) because of their own IP enforcement.
- **For all options:** run it as a closed, login-protected or unlisted app for friends. Add an Impressum only if the site is "geschäftsmäßig" (business-like) or public (a German Telemedien/DDG question, not researched here). Keep logs and data minimal (GDPR, not researched here).

### Gaps
- Impressum (DDG) and GDPR obligations for a private hobby web app were out of scope and not researched.
- Whether a closed friends-only deployment is legally "public" (öffentliche Zugänglichmachung, §19a UrhG) was not researched.
